## How to get realistic questions

The quality of AI-written questions depends almost entirely on what you tell it. Specify these and the output improves a lot:

- **Exam and style.** Step 1 asks for mechanisms; Step 2 CK asks for the next best step; shelf exams are clerkship-specific; OSCEs need a whole case, not a multiple-choice item.
- **Your phase.** A pre-clerkship student needs one concept at a time. A clerkship student needs diagnosis and management. An advanced student needs ambiguity and close distractors.
- **Difficulty.** Ask for foundational, applied, or challenging, and say what each means (the Question Builder does this for you).
- **Format.** One at a time with feedback, or a full set with the answers hidden.
- **Your source.** Paste your notes and say "use only these" to get questions that match your course.

## Common pitfalls

- **Do not trust every fact.** AI can write a convincing question with a wrong premise. If something surprises you, check a trusted source before you memorize it.
- **Answer before you look.** Reading the answer first turns practice into passive review. Commit to an answer, then read the feedback.
- **Do not paste real exam or question-bank content.** Ask for original questions instead. Licensed content should not be pasted into AI tools.
- **No patient information.** Use fictional or fully de-identified cases only.
- **Vague requests produce vague questions.** "Give me questions on cardiology" returns trivia. Name the exam, the phase, the subtopic, and the difficulty.
- **Feed the misses back in.** The best sessions end with "write new questions on what I missed," not with moving on.
