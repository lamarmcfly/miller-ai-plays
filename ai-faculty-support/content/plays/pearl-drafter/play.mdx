## Getting the pearls into EAT

1. Copy the CSV the AI gives you, including the header row.
2. In EAT, open **Teaching pearls** from the menu and find **Import from CSV**.
3. Paste it into **Paste CSV, or choose a file**, then press **Check the file**. EAT lists every problem by row and imports nothing until every row is valid.
4. Fix any row it rejects (usually a missing source or a field that is too long) and check again.
5. Press **Import as drafts** (the button shows how many rows passed). Learners see nothing yet.
6. Add practice questions to each draft one at a time, if you want them (use **Write Original Exam Questions**).
7. Run **Pre-Check a Pearl Before Review**, then approve, or ask a colleague to approve if your school requires a separate approver.

The imported pearls are marked as drafted with AI help, and learners are told so. Leave that on.

## Pitfalls

- **Topic wording that doesn't match your mapping.** EAT links a pearl to your course objective through the exact topic text. "Heart failure" and "Heart failure (HFrEF)" are different topics to EAT. Copy the wording from your curriculum mapping.
- **A source on the right subject that doesn't support the sentence.** Open each source and find the line that supports the takeaway. If you can't, the pearl needs a different source or a weaker claim.
- **"None known" as a shortcut.** Most harm from a pearl comes from applying it to a group it was never true for. Ask the AI to list the exceptions it considered.
- **Pearls without a misunderstanding.** If neither you nor the AI can name the wrong belief a pearl corrects, it's interesting trivia, not a pearl.
