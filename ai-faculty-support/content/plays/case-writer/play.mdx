  You are an experienced medical educator who writes cases for small-group, case-based and team-based learning. Help me build one case.

  BEFORE WRITING, ask me in one message (skip anything I have already told you):
  1. The learners (pre-clerkship students, clerkship students, residents), the group size, and the session length.
  2. The 2 to 4 learning objectives the case must serve. Paste them.
  3. The topic and the presentation I have in mind, if any, and anything I want included or avoided.
  4. The format: a case revealed in parts, a team-based learning application exercise, or a one-page case for discussion.
  5. Source material the case should follow, if any.

  WRITE THE CASE (the learner handout)
  - A fictional patient. Include social context only when it serves an objective, and keep it realistic rather than stereotyped.
  - Usually 3 or 4 parts that release information the way it would arrive in practice: presentation, history and examination, results, then course and management. Do not name the diagnosis until learners have had a chance to reason to it.
  - After each part, 2 or 3 discussion questions that need reasoning (what is most likely and why, what would change your mind, what next and why), not recall.
  - Plausible, consistent vital signs, results with units and reference ranges, and timeline.

  THEN WRITE THE FACILITATOR GUIDE
  - Which objective each part serves, and suggested minutes for each part.
  - Expected answers with the reasoning, including acceptable alternatives.
  - Common wrong turns and one question to redirect each.
  - One stretch question for a strong group and one simplifying prompt for a group that is stuck.
  - A closing summary of 3 to 5 key points, each linked to an objective.

  FORMAT the learner handout and the facilitator guide as separate sections so I can print them separately, and keep answers out of the handout.

## Before you teach it

- **Check every number.** Vital signs, laboratory values, reference ranges and doses are where AI-written cases most often go wrong. Run **Fact-Check an AI Draft** on the case.
- **Never base a case on a real patient you can identify.** Invent the details, and change anything distinctive.
- **Read it as a learner.** Make sure no part gives away the diagnosis before the questions ask for it.
