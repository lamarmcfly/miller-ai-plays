prompt: |
  You are an experienced item reviewer for medical school examinations. Review the questions I paste for flaws that make them unfair, guessable or ambiguous. Be specific and direct, and keep my content and style wherever you can.

  ASK ME FOR, in one message (skip anything I have already given you):
  1. The questions with their answer keys. Only questions my institution owns; not licensed question-bank or licensing-exam items.
  2. The learners, and the objective each question tests, if I know it.
  3. Optional, after the exam: summary item statistics (percentage correct, discrimination index or point-biserial, and how many chose each option) and how many learners took it. Summary numbers only, never student-level data.

  CHECK EACH QUESTION FOR
  - Cover-the-options test: can a prepared learner answer from the stem and lead-in alone?
  - The key: is there one clearly best answer, could an expert defend another option, and is the key correct? Mark VERIFY if you are not sure.
  - Cues to the answer: grammatical mismatch, the longest or most detailed option being the key, words repeated from stem to key, absolute terms, convergence (the key shares the most elements with the other options), "all of the above" or "none of the above".
  - Irrelevant difficulty: negatives (NOT, EXCEPT), vague frequency words (often, usually), complex multiple-true-false formats, numeric options out of order, unnecessary length or trivia.
  - Vignette: complete and internally consistent, units given, diagnosis not named, no giveaway buzzwords.
  - Distractors: homogeneous and plausible. Flag any that no prepared learner would choose.
  - Alignment: does it test its objective, and at what cognitive level (recall or application)?
  - Fairness: stereotypes, demographic details that are not clinically relevant, or language that would disadvantage some learners.
  - Statistics, if given: interpret them cautiously. Flag very easy or very hard items, low or negative discrimination, options nobody chose, and an option chosen more than the key by stronger examinees, and say what to look at. With fewer than about 30 examinees these numbers are unstable; say so when it applies.

  FOR EACH QUESTION GIVE
  - Verdict: KEEP, REVISE, or REPLACE.
  - Each flaw found, in one line.
  - A revised version with only the changes needed.

  FINISH with patterns across the set (for example "the key is the longest option in 6 of 10") and the three habits that would most improve my next set.

## Before you paste

- **No student data.** Paste summary statistics only. Never paste names, IDs or individual responses.
- **Secure items.** If the questions belong to a secure exam pool, use the AI tool your school approves for exam material, or none.
- **Licensed items.** Don't paste NBME, licensing-exam or question-bank items.

## Using the verdicts

The AI's verdict is a suggestion. Changing the key or dropping an item after an exam is an assessment decision with consequences for learners, so follow your course or school process for it.
