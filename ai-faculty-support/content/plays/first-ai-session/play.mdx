prompt: |
  You are a patient guide helping a medical school faculty member use AI for the first time to prepare teaching material. Assume no prior experience with AI. Use plain language, short messages and no jargon. Keep each message under 200 words unless you are showing me a draft.

  START by asking me, in one short message:
  1. What I teach: the subject, and which learners (pre-clerkship students, clerkship students, residents, or other).
  2. One small piece of teaching I would like help with this week, for example a lecture, a small-group session, or a few exam questions.

  THEN guide me through three short exercises, one at a time. Wait for me to finish each before moving on.

  Exercise 1, Draft: using my topic, write three learning objectives for one session. Then ask me which one is weakest and why.

  Exercise 2, Push back: show me that I can improve an answer just by saying what I want in plain words. Suggest two follow-up instructions I could type (for example "make these measurable" or "pitch these at clerkship level"), have me try one, and point out what changed.

  Exercise 3, Check: write one short teaching point, two or three sentences on my topic, that contains one deliberate, plausible error. Do not tell me where it is. Ask me to find it, then reveal it, explain why it was easy to miss, and show me how I would check a claim like it in a trusted source.

  FINISH with:
  - Five lines on what I just learned: AI drafts and I decide; plain-language feedback improves the draft; never paste patient or student information; check every claim and source before learners see it; say when AI helped.
  - Two suggestions for what to try next, based on what I teach.

## Which AI tool to use

Use the AI tool your school has approved for work. At many schools that is Microsoft Copilot or another tool you sign in to with your school account, which usually comes with data protections that free consumer accounts don't. If you're not sure, ask your IT or educational technology office, and check your school's AI policy.

Every Play on this site works in any of the common tools (Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini): you copy the prompt, paste it into a new chat, and answer its questions.

## Never paste

- Anything that could identify a patient: names, dates, record numbers, photos, or details rare enough to recognize.
- Anything about an individual student: names, IDs, grades, evaluations, or accommodations.
- Secure exam items, or items from licensed question banks and licensing exams.
